# Changelog

What changed in the cells language, newest first: what a frontend (Cell studio, Jambot) reads before it moves its pin, `github:yggi/cells#vX.Y.Z`.
A release is a tag, and it gathers the entries under *Unreleased* beneath its own heading.
An entry says whether a piece reads and sounds as before; one that breaks a piece names its migration (Shorten writes migrations), and before 1.0 it makes the next release a minor version.

Format: [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/), as Cell studio's (lifted from yggi/cell-studio CHANGELOG.md @ 6de7ce4, its form). Add a line to *Unreleased* in the pull request that makes the change.

## Unreleased

### Changed
- **S1 and S2 read a voice by what it sounds like, not by its place** (#192, decision 057, amending 050's point on #186): `soundsIdentical`, `playsSameNotes` and `samePiece` key a voice on its cell's attributes that shape its sound (its waveform, static parameters, envelope, `fm=` operator…, the cells it names read the same way), with no name. A voice renamed, two voices' definitions swapped with their names, and two voices alike merged into one now sound identical; two voices' parts swapped still don't, unless the voices sound alike. On a MIDI card each event's channel and a kit member's drum note count as well, so a rewrite that moves either is no longer S1 (the rest of a MIDI card is #273). **Shorten's `same` merges two voices written alike** where S1 holds and the piece's MIDI export is the same (its tracks' names aside), so two parts stay apart, each on its channel. Over the examples it offers two more merges (`phase`'s and `tines`' FM operators, 19 characters each). **Fixed with it:** S1 no longer passes a rewrite that changes a voice's own sound, which it passed before because no event carries it (`&v s=saw` to `&v s=sine`, a voice's `lpf=` changed or dropped). **What a frontend sees at its next pin:** a frontend that calls S1 or S2 gets "the same" for a renaming, a reordering or a merge of voices alike, and "not the same" for a voice's sound changed, or a channel or drum note moved on a MIDI card. Two white-noise stand-ins (`&hh s=white`, `&oh s=white`) are one sound. Recognition and Vary keep their own reading of names (decision 055). No piece sounds different, and no event snapshot moves.

## v0.10.1 — 2026-10-23

### Added
- **Vary bends and trims a pitched line's last note** (#267, yggi/jambot#424, decision 052): two pitched operators, after reverse in `PITCHED_OPERATORS`. **`bend`** moves the last note to 1 or 2 degrees either side of the note before it (`BENDS`, −2, −1, 1, 2: `bend &lead 2`); **`trim`** leaves the last note out, the note before it then held (`trim &lead 12`), where the line keeps 2 notes (`TRIM_FEWEST`). Neither touches an anchored last note (a root on a chord change), and a result that plays its source's notes, or an earlier variation's, is left out as before. **What a frontend sees at its next pin:** one that passes `PITCHED_OPERATORS` gets more variations, after the ones it got, which keep their order (each bass seed but the root bass gains 4 bends and a trim, 20 in all; Jambot's bass passes it, so its E41 numbers move, and a draw lands on another index); one that names its operators, or passes `RHYTHM_OPERATORS`, gets what it got. Recognition's default operators (`ALL_OPERATORS`) hold the two, so a heard bass or lead one bend or trim from a fragment is recognised as it, by that operator, at distance 0 (the bass seeds' 44 variations are). No piece sounds different.
- **The engine host and Shorten take the resolver** (#241, decision 054): `planOf(code, env, { resolve, locked })`; `createHost(now, { resolve })`, made with the resolver (a function can't cross `postMessage`), and a `load` message's `locked`, the lockfile's hashes; `shorten(source, { resolve, locked })` and `shortenAll(source, rounds, { resolve, locked })`. A piece with `use "core kit"` loads in a host made with `resolveSeed` (it loaded as `ok: false` before), and Shorten offers for it what it offers for the piece written out (`expand`), less any rewrite of a used line. Called as before, each does what it did.

## v0.10.0 — 2026-10-22

### Changed
- **The rhythms, the bell and scale and the bass lines no longer carry a voice of their own: each is a hole an instrument fills** (decision 054's rung 2, #244; the owner's ruling, 2026-10-22). Gone: the eight drum bars' 30 stand-ins (`&bd s=white`, `&sd s=white`, `&hh s=white`, `&rim` `&oh` `&rd` `&ph`: bare white noise, one sound for a kick and a hat alike), the five claves' `&claves s=sine loop=D7 …`, the bell and scale's `&highagogo s=tri loop=A5 …` and the five bass lines' `&bassV loop=C2`. The core kit fills the drums and the claves, the General MIDI kit the bell's agogô, and a new instrument fragment, **`bass`** (`fragments/instruments/03-bass.md`, the first of rung 7; `INSTRUMENT_FRAGMENTS` and `LIBRARY` hold it, forty-four), defines `&bassV loop=C2`, so the bass lines' MIDI notes on channel 2 don't move. **What a frontend sees at its next pin:** every drum, clave, bell and bass fragment still plays the same notes, the same onsets, lengths and General MIDI drum notes, over MIDI out (Jambot's drummer and bass); **S1 is not kept**: where something renders the fragment's own sound (Cell studio's library and preview) the drums now sound as the core kit's voices, not white noise. **A fragment with holes no longer compiles alone** (`Unknown cell &bd`): Jambot's section text must bring the instruments (`use "core kit"` and `use "bass"`, or the preset's own kit; yggi/jambot#420's rung 4), and Cell studio's library preview needs a default workbench with the core kit (it resolves `use` through the seed library already). A fragment still fits any workbench with a meter and, for the bass, a harmony: `fitOf(...).sounds` names the instrument to ask for next. A heard text that defines its own `&bd s=white` and is recognised against a workbench that has the kit defines `&bd` twice; recognise it against a workbench without the kit's lines, or drop the text's.
- **`holesOf` and `fitOf` read a fragment through its sound holes**: a line that strikes a hole doesn't compile until an instrument fills it, and the compile says one unknown cell a line, so `holesOf` stands each sound hole in by a voice (`standIn`, `&name s=white`, exported from `@yggi/cells/repository/holes`) and asks again until none is left: `uses` and `sounds` hold every name a fragment strikes (`&oh` after `&hh`), and a line's clock need is read through them (a two-bar clave needs bars of 16 sixteenths, with no kit). `fitOf` does the same for the workbench's own holes, a groove added before its kit, and leaves the stand-ins' "only on a MIDI card" warning out of the fit's diagnostics. A fragment with no sound hole reads as before.

### Added
- A new tutorial piece, `breve` (tier 4), shows `beat=` and a scan's `pulse=`; `PresetKey` gains `'breve'`. In-c's `[0,1] [0,1]` is written `[0,1]!2`. No piece reads or sounds differently.
- `CompileResult.unknown`: the cells a text uses and declares nowhere (`{ name, at }`, each with its uses), as data; the holes and the fit read it, no longer the `Unknown cell &x` message. Additive: no message, diagnostic or event moves, every piece reads and sounds as before.

### Fixed
- **Shorten no longer rewrites a bare `swing=` that leans nothing** (#166): on a meter whose own steps are its beats (`meter("4/4")`), it offered `swing=4n:.67`, which plays the same and then warns "leans nothing". The rewrite is left out and the compiler's warning stands for the author (it names `swing=8n:`, which would change the notes). No piece's notes change.

## v0.9.0 — 2026-10-22

### Added
- **`use "core kit"`: a fragment taken into a piece by its id** (decision 054's rung 3, #232; `docs/language/use.md`). A line of its own; what compiles is the expansion, the piece's own lines then each used fragment's lines the piece doesn't define, in order, so the piece's line wins. Two used fragments defining one name by different lines, a cycle, a depth past 8 and an id the library doesn't hold are errors, each said on the `use` line; an id used twice gives its lines once, and the second `use` warns. Cells resolves nothing itself: **`compileSource(text, env, { resolve, locked })`** takes the host's resolver (`Resolve`, from an id and a recorded hash to a fragment's text) and the lockfile's hashes; **`resolveSeed`** is the seed library's. **`CompileResult.uses`** says what each `use` resolved to (its `e1:` hash), gave and had overridden; **`expand(text, resolve)`** writes the expansion out; **`fragmentHash`** is the hash. A piece file's frontmatter may hold a lockfile, **`uses:`** (a list of `id` and `hash`): `lockfileOf` reads it, `withLockfile` records a compile's, and `writePiece` drops an entry no `use` names. The built-in card (decision 028) doesn't hear a used fragment's voices. `normalise`, `rewriteAll`, `soundsIdentical` and `playsSameNotes` take the same options, and the structure measure counts a `use` line as one leaf. **`holesOf` gives `sounds`**, the holes read only as a sound (a braid's step, a mix's term), and **`fitOf` gives `sounds`** too: such a hole the workbench doesn't define no longer blocks the fit, and is no longer in `missing` (none of the seed's fragments has one yet). Given a resolver (`holesOf(text, compiled, resolve)`, `fitOf(fragment, workbench, compiled, resolve)`), a text with `use` is judged over its expansion: a used fragment's holes, sounds and clock needs are its own. A text without `use` compiles as before; no example's or fragment's notes change. A text that started a line with `use` was an error before, and is one still without a resolver.
- **Recognition** (decision 055, #224, yggi/jambot#237): `createRecogniser()` (`@yggi/cells/repository/recognise`, and in `index.ts`) returns a recogniser whose `recognise(text, library, options)` gives the library's fragments a heard text is within `tolerance` notes of (by default 0), nearest first, each by its nearest path of Vary's operators (none, or one; two with `depth: 2`), the bar of the fragment it was matched from (a heard bar of a two-bar clave or an eight-bar vamp against each of its bars) and its distance: the notes that differ, S2 as Vary reads them (onset, length, the drum's General MIDI note, a pitched note's pitch with its voice by its place, so a renamed bass is the same; levels free). A harmony the fragment defines (`&harm`) counts its chords as notes. The library is whatever the frontend passes, the seed and its learned layer merged. On a warmed recogniser a recognition compiles only the heard text. Jambot's 284 variations on E31's bars and the bass seeds' 24 are each recognised as their source's, by their operator, at distance 0; none of 1,000 random bars is. `readVariations` (`engine/vary`) gives `variations` with each one's notes, and the span and bar they come round after.
- **The modes** (#228, decision 056, yggi/jambot#420's rung 4): `MODE_FRAGMENTS` (`@yggi/cells/repository/modes`, and in `index.ts`), five seed fragments in `fragments/modes/` under a new facet, `mode`, each defining `key`, the main key the harmonies read, as `&key scale(mode, free(C))`: the mode stated, the tonic left to the host (a `free`, which Jambot's key plan sets). Aeolian, dorian and ionian sit at the point of each of Jambot's presets that plays them, so a query for the mode at a preset finds its mode first, at distance 0 (8 of 8); mixolydian, which no preset plays, halfway between Funk's and Bossa nova's. The fifth, **the bell and scale** (from the example), is the first fragment of two facets: `&key scale(ionian, free(C)) step=1m*7` is the major scale and, stepped, the West African bell's timeline, struck on the General MIDI kit's high agogô (67) on channel 10; a `mode` where the major is played and a `rhythm` where a timeline is. Every harmony and bass line fits a workbench with each and compiles with no diagnostic. `LIBRARY` is forty-three; a query for the meter, the bass, the harmony or the instrument returns what it did, and one for the rhythm finds the bell among the rhythms (never first at a preset). No example's or fragment's notes change.
- **The General MIDI drum kit as seed fragments** (decision 054's rung 1, #225, #178): `fragments/instruments/`, the facet `instrument`, two fragments that define voices and use nothing, so each fits any workbench and fills the holes a groove leaves: **`gm kit`**, a voice for each of General MIDI's 47 percussion notes (35–81), and **`core kit`**, the 13 a groove needs (`bd rim sd cp lt hh ph mt oh ht cr rd claves`), the same lines. Each sound is a first pass in cells' own parameters, to be judged by ear in Cell studio. Both sit once at the centre of style space, the core kit at twice the weight, so a query for an instrument finds it first. `INSTRUMENT_FRAGMENTS` (in `index.ts`) and `LIBRARY` hold them.
- **All 47 General MIDI drum notes have names** (`GM_KIT` in `@yggi/cells/engine/midi`, which replaces `GM_DRUMS`): Strudel's short name where it has one, General MIDI's name spelled as one word as its alias (`cb`/`cowbell` 56, `tb`/`tambourine` 54, `sh`/`maracas` 70, `lowconga` 64, `crashcymbaltwo` 57 …), 75 names in all, listed in `docs/language/midi.md`. No name that had a note moves, and no example's MIDI out changes; a kit member newly named so (`cb`, `tb`, `sh`, a Latin drum, or one of them with a trailing number, `tb303`) is now sent on its General MIDI note instead of the next free one from 36.

### Changed
- **Opening a MIDI file writes every drum note as a voice of its own** (#178): `cellsFromMidi` writes each of General MIDI's 47 drum notes under its kit name (`GM_KIT`) with the seed General MIDI kit's sound for it, both read from `GM_KIT` and the `gm kit` fragment, and MIDI out sends each back on the note it came in on. The 24 notes it dropped before (the tambourine, cowbell, vibraslap, and the hand percussion 60–74 and 76–81) are written now. **What an opened file writes changes**: the toms, crash and ride take the kit's names (`&lt` `&mt` `&ht` `&cr` `&rd`, not `&lo` `&mid` `&hi` `&crash` `&ride`), and the 11 lesser drums it wrote as the one beside them are their own (35 `&acousticbassdrum`, 40 `&electricsnare`, 43 `&highfloortom`, 44 `&ph`, 47 `&lowmidtom`, 50 `&hightom`, 52 `&chinesecymbal`, 53 `&ridebell`, 55 `&splashcymbal`, 57 `&crashcymbaltwo`, 59 `&ridecymbaltwo`), each with its own sound. A piece already written doesn't change; a file opened again does. A drum track the app saved, reopened, now gives its members with a General MIDI name (`cb`, `sh`, `tb`, the Latin drums) the kit's sound at their pitch (`cb(G5)` as `s=square loop=G5 lpf=2500 …`, where it was a sine), and those names are reserved, so a track named `cb` is written `&cb2`.

### Fixed
- **Vary reads back what its pitched operators write** (#224): a pitched line's step `degree(&bassNext^$-1, -2)` or `inverse(&bassNext)`, a splice moved or mirrored, reads as the note it plays, so a transposed or inverted bass varies again, and is recognised. `variations` of every seed gives what it did; nothing a piece plays changes.

## v0.8.0 — 2026-10-20

### Added
- **Vary, the rhythm operators** (decision 052's rung 1, #208): `@yggi/cells/engine/vary/index` (and `variations`, `varied`, `anchorsOf` in `index.ts`) gives every variation of a fragment by one operator on one line, in Jambot's order: **rotate** (every note a step later, the last to the first; a Euclid turned), **drop** (one note left out, where the fragment keeps `fewest`) and **add** (a note of the voice the line strikes first, on a step it is silent on, at the line's loudest). Each is a `Rewrite` of a fifth tool, `vary` (`Tool` gains it), with `line` and `by` (`drop &hat 6`, for a frontmatter's `by:`), that plays other notes than its source and every one before it; `rewriteAll` never takes one. The anchors are derived from the text on its workbench (`anchorsOf`: the line that strikes only the kick, General MIDI's 36, and the one that strikes only the snare on the meter's backbeat, `backbeatOf`, lifted from Jambot), never varied, beside the frontend's `keep`; `varied(text, draw)` takes the variation at ⌊draw · n⌋, as Jambot's `pick(draw, ones)`. On E31's eight drum bars it gives Jambot's 284 variations hit for hit and in order. A line left with no note leaves its card, its cell kept. Nothing a piece plays changes.
- **Vary, the pitched operators** (decision 052's rung 2, #208, yggi/jambot#423): `variations` and `varied` with `operators: PITCHED_OPERATORS` vary a pitched line (a cell of degrees of the table it reads, its steps rests, whole numbers or a splice, times one voice: `&bass * &bassV`) by **transpose** (every note n degrees of its table, n each of `degrees`, by default −2, −1, 1, 2, as `&x^n` plays them; a splice moved as `&bassNext^n` or `degree(&bassNext^$-1, n)`), **invert** (mirrored about the first note f, as `degree(inverse(&x), 2f)`) and **reverse** (each span between anchors in reverse order, as `&x[::-1]`). `anchorsOf` adds the **root on a chord change**: a note of a line over a harmony (a braid of stacks) whose onset is where the chord in force changes or its lap starts and which plays that chord's root, kept in its place and its pitch (`Anchor.step`, `reason: 'root'`). On the five bass seeds, 24 variations of the 30, the 6 left out playing their source's notes. The rhythm operators still act on lines of hits only, so `variations` by default gives what it did; nothing a piece plays changes.
- **The harmonies** (#219, yggi/jambot#425, the first step of decision 052's rung 3): `HARMONY_FRAGMENTS` (`@yggi/cells/repository/harmony`, and in `index.ts`), five seed fragments in `fragments/harmony/`, the vamps Jambot's themes play at low harmony (`themes.ts`'s `VAMPS`) as eight bars of triads over the host's key, a chord per four bars as `buildTheme` lays them under harm 0.15: the tonic alone, and the tonic then the seventh, the fourth, the sixth or the fifth (`&harm over=key step=1m cycle=8 [0,2,4]!4 [6,8,10]!4`). The mode is the host key's, so the twelve vamps of aeolian, dorian, ionian and mixolydian are these five texts, each associated as a new facet, `harmony`, at the point of each preset whose mode plays it (aeolian at Techno's, dorian at Funk's, ionian at Ambient's with its harmony at 0.12; no preset is mixolydian). Each defines `&harm`, the chord in force the bass lines read, and has no card. `LIBRARY` is thirty-six; a query for the meter, the rhythm or the bass returns what it did, and no example's or fragment's notes change.
- **The bass lines** (#207, yggi/jambot#423): `BASS_FRAGMENTS` (`@yggi/cells/repository/bass`, and in `index.ts`), five seed fragments in `fragments/bass/`, Jambot's bass patterns (`bass.ts` there) as lines of indices into the chord in force: the root on one and three, the pump, the eighths, the funk line (anticipating the next chord, `&harm[>>]`) and the walk (into the next chord from a semitone below, `&bassNext^$-1`). Each reads the host's `&harm` and taps its meter, its card on channel 2, at the point where `bass.ts` plays its pattern most and at each of Jambot's presets where it plays it most (#209: the root at Synth-pop, Deep house and Ambient, the pump at Techno, the funk line at Lo-fi hip hop, Funk and Bossa nova, the walk at Swing jazz), associated as a new facet, `bass`, so a query for the bass at a preset finds that pattern first and the walk is first only near Swing jazz. `LIBRARY` is thirty-one; a query for the meter or the rhythm returns what it did, and no example's or fragment's notes change.
- **The pairs alone, `swing=8n:r:pairs`** (#204, decision 053, part of #18): a word after a level's share leans that level's pairs and keeps its groups of three straight, as a band swinging a mixed meter does (jazz's, Jambot's T16): `meter("7/8") swing=8n:2/3:pairs` swings the eighths of its two-eighth beats and plays its first beat's three evenly, its eighths at 0, 1, 2, 3, 4⅓, 5 and 6⅓ eighths, Jambot's onsets. A pair leans exactly as `swing=8n:r` leans it, a three plays at thirds of its beat exactly; `swing=8n:2/3:pairs,16n:.55` leans each level as it says. Where every group of the level is three (6/8's eighths) it leans nothing, with a warning; another word after a share, or `:pairs` on `legato=`, is an error. Without the word nothing changes: a group of three still leans as decision 040 says, and no example's or fragment's notes move.

### Fixed

- **A Markdown file of cells blocks with no frontmatter reads as a literate piece** (#212, decision 051 amended): `isPieceFile` is true for it and `readPiece` reads it with an empty frontmatter, its blocks joined the text and the rest prose, where it asked for frontmatter and a frontend opened the file as raw code, fences and prose included, which didn't compile. `writePiece` of such a file writes it back byte for byte, without a frontmatter while it has none (`PieceFile.bare`). A file with neither frontmatter nor a cells block reads as before (raw code, or the diagnostic `no frontmatter`). Every example and fragment has frontmatter and reads, writes and sounds as before.
- **`shiftsAt` turns a played cell only when a line reads through it** (#203, the fix of #171's reach): since v0.6.0 it turned every stepped played braid, so a reader of `LiveState.shifts` saw a played braid's shift count its own steps (Drums' `groove` read 5 two seconds in, written 0). Now a played cell turns only when something reads through it (an `over=`, the table a cell is shown through, a grid, a relative pick `&k[0]`), as #167 said; the turning tables turn as before. `readThrough(result)` (`engine/clocking`) is that set; `playedCells` is cached and returns a `ReadonlySet`. Over the 78 examples, 15 cells' shifts move in `shiftsAt` where 212 did, none of them played. No note moves (the event snapshot doesn't): a played cell's own notes read their written shift (`playing`) either way. A frontend that drew a played wheel with `playing()`'s split to work round it (Cell studio's `drawnShifts`) can read `LiveState.shifts` as it stands.

## v0.7.0 — 2026-10-20

### Added
- **Literate cells** (#195, decision 051): a piece file's or fragment file's code is the join of all its ```` ```cells ```` blocks, in order, and everything else is prose, where a file held one block and refused prose before it. `readPiece` and `readFragment` read it, with prose and any number of blocks between, and the optional `map` (each line of `text` to its line in the file) and `layout` on what they return; `writePiece` and `writeFragment` of a file that was read put a changed `text` back into the blocks its lines came from and keep the rest byte for byte. `@yggi/cells/engine/literate` exports the reader (`readLiterate`, `readBlocks`, `fileLine`, `rewrite`). Every example and fragment is one block and reads, writes and sounds as before.
- **A clave of its own** (#140, the owner's ruling on yggi/jambot#397): a kit member named `clave` (Strudel's name) or `claves` (General MIDI's) goes out as General MIDI's claves, 75, where it took the next free note from 36, which hung on the rest of its kit; the drum names and their notes are listed in `docs/language/midi.md`. BWV 847: 1,396 as the generator wrote it, 1,125 by hand. Nothing a piece plays changes.

### Changed
- **A negative gain is phase inversion** (#193, the owner's ruling on #189's question): `gain=-1` on a voice plays it at full level, upside down; `* -.5` in a product is half the level, inverted; the card's `postgain=` below 0 inverts everything it plays. none of the three warns (a voice's `gain=`, the card's `postgain=` and a factor in a product below 0); a gate's negative `level=` warns, since a gate's level is how far it opens. An event's `gains` now carry the sign through `plan`/`eventsBetween` (a voice's negative `gain=` played at `1`, and an output with negative gains played nothing), so `soundsIdentical` tells `gain=.5` from `gain=-.5` and `playsSameNotes` doesn't; a voice and its inversion at equal gains cancel, and nothing is heard. - a swung meter's own steps are `Clock.swung`, no longer `Clock.swing`;
- `clip=` warns.

### Changed
- **Swing on the bar** (#18's first rung, rung 2 of #81): `swing=8n:.67` on a meter leans one level of its bar, named by note value: of each pair of eighths the second starts .67 of the way through their beat, a group of three (6/8, 7/8's first beat) leans its second and third, and every beat and bar line stays where it is, so an odd bar no longer drifts. Taps are placed through it, whatever the meter's own steps (`meter("4/4") swing=8n:.67` swings `step=8n` and `step=pulse*2`); a division it doesn't hold stays even (`8t` under swung eighths). It swings a `scan(…)` too, which used to warn. Levels nest, coarse to fine: `swing=8n:.6,16n:.55`, which places Jambot's swing axis to 1e-9 (`fixtures/jambot-swing.json`). A level no beat divides into (`swing=4n:.67` in 4/4) leans nothing, with a warning.
- **A bare `swing=.67` on a meter warns**: it reads as the level of the meter cell's own steps (`meter("4/4", 2) swing=.67` is `swing=8n:.67`, and plays as before), and Shorten names the level. On a list stepping at a rate it keeps its meaning, pairs of its steps. A bare swing that paired steps across a beat or a bar line plays differently: `meter("7/8") swing=.67` now leans each beat (it drifted across odd bars), `meter("4/4", 3) swing=.67` leans each beat's triplets (it paired them across beats). The Swing and Metronome examples are respelled (`swing=8n:.67`, `swing=8n:free(.5, .5...75)`) and play the same at their defaults; the Metronome's slider now leans eighths on every grid (it paired the grid's own steps: quarters, triplets across beats, sixteenths).
- **`Clock.swung`** holds a swung meter's own steps (in its steps, a bar's); `Clock.swing` is only a list's pairs now. `Meter.swing` holds the levels (`SwingLevel`), `swingAt(meter, pulses)` places a position, and a decl's `swing` holds the levels as written. A tap reads its meter unswung and carries the swing in its positions.

### Added
- **`legato=`: a line's articulation** (#94, rung 9 of #81), Strudel's `legato` (`clip=` reads as it): each note sounds its held length (its step, `@n`, `_`) times a share, `.5` staccato, `1.5` into the next note. A number, `free(…)` or a cell of shares on its own tap, read at each note's onset; nothing cuts a note at the bar line, so a share over 1 sounds past it, whole, in the events and in MIDI out. A share of 0 or less is an error. Absent is 1: every piece reads and sounds as before. With `glide=`, notes tie where they meet as they sound. E33's 384 bars, stated with it, play every bass, pad and stab note at Jambot's articulated length within 1e-6 steps (`fixtures/jambot-legato.json`, lifted from yggi/jambot).
- **`meter("10/8")`** (#97): a signature grouped 3+2+3+2 (10 pulses, four beats of 3, 2, 3 and 2 eighths), as the owner ruled; at 90 bpm its beats last 3/10, 2/10, 3/10 and 2/10 of the bar. The repository's 10/8 fragment is now `meter("10/8")` and plays the same events as the levels it was written as. Nothing a piece reads or plays changes.
- **A time parameter reads a tap as a length** (#87): `delaytime=8n` is an eighth at the meter's tempo (.25 s at 120 bpm), `delaytime=4n.` .75 s, `delaytime=&bar2:8n` against another meter, and the same for a tape's `length=` and a head's `at=` (every parameter whose unit is a time). A note value is its own length (a dotted quarter is .75 s at 120 bpm in 4/4, where the bar line cuts its step); a pulse, a beat or the bar is the mean of its steps (`beat` in 7/8 as 3+2+2 at 90 bpm is 2/3 s). Fixed at the tempo when compiled, as `delaytime=&bar*4/3` is. Text that was an error is read; nothing that played changes.
- **v0.3.0 leftovers** (#92); none changes what a piece plays. **Shorten's `fractions` sees a tap**: `&t step=8n 4@2 2@2 0@2 5@2` offers `step=4n 4 2 0 5` (a note value, a triplet, `pulse*k`, `beat*k` and `1m/k` slow as written), and a line still stepped by a meter's own steps (`step=&bar*2`) is offered the tap (`step=4n`), not the old spelling that warns. **A tap on a belt names itself on its wire**: `step=8n belt=3000` is labelled `8n · belt` (`TransformStep` `rate` gains `tap`, the tap as written), as `step=&bar*2 belt=3000` was `×2 · belt`; a plain tap's wire stays unlabelled. **`SAILOR_WITH_KIT`** writes `step=8n`, so compiling it no longer warns three times.
- **A swing share as a fraction** (#101, decision 042): `swing=8n:2/3` is the exact triplet swing (`.6666666666666666`), `swing=16n:31/60` is `.5166666666666667`; a share above 1 or over a zero denominator fails at the fraction. A decimal reads as before, so no piece changes; the swung meters are written with their fractions and play the same. A bare `swing=` and a `free(…)` share are decimals still.
- **A tap written outside `step=` says so** (#103): `[&bar:16n * &tick]`, `&x 0 &bar:beat` or `over=&bar:8n` gave "&bar:16 would be a pitched hit", naming a cell the text doesn't have; the diagnostic now says that `&bar:16n` is a meter's tap which only `step=` reads, at the reference. Text that was an error stays an error; nothing that plays changes.
- **`legato=`: a line's articulation** (#94, rung 9 of #81), Strudel's `legato` (`clip=` reads as it, with a warning: write `legato=`, and Normalise rewrites it): each note sounds its held length (its step, `@n`, `_`) times a share, `.5` staccato, `1.5` into the next note. - **A meter's articulation, `legato=16n:.5` by level** (#94, the owner's option 3 tried): a line that taps the meter and states no `legato=` sounds its notes at the share of its tap's level, the finest at or above its step (`legato=16n:.5,4n:.8`); a line's own wins. With `swing=`, one meter fragment carries a feel. Its swing, its taps and its own steps are as without it; `Meter.legato` and `tapShare(meter, tap, ratio)` are new.
- **A first repository: fragments found by their place in style space** (#82, decision 041, rung 5 of #81). `createRepository(fragments)` holds fragments, each cells text with an id and an association (weighted style points, axis names to numbers), and throws on a malformed entry; `query(point, k, axisWeights?)` returns the k nearest, ranked by a weighted distance over the axes the point and each association share, so an axis no fragment has is ignored. `METER_FRAGMENTS` are its first library, nine meters that need no swing (4/4, 4/4 alla breve, 2/4, 3/4, 6/8, 12/8, 5/4, 7/8 as 3+2+2, 10/8 as 3+2+3+2), each one line `&bar meter(…)` with no tempo, associated by hand with Jambot's eight presets (all of which find 4/4 until the swung meters land). ### Changed
- **Taps by pulse, beat and note value; a tap comes round at the bar** (#74, decision 040, rung 1 of #15). A compiled `Meter` knows its pulse (`pulses`, the numerator), its beats (`beats`, each in pulses: 6/8 `3 3`, 7/8 `3 2 2`) and the note value of a pulse (`unit`, the denominator; a scan's is 4). A line taps it: `step=pulse`, `step=pulse*k`, `step=beat`, `step=beat*k`, note values `1n` … `32n`, triplets `4t 8t 16t`, dotted `2n. 4n. 8n.`, the bar `1m`, with a chain as on any clock (`1m/4`); of the main meter (the first in the text with a tempo of its own, `CompileResult.mainMeter`) or a named one (`step=&bar2:16n`). A note value longer than what is left of the bar is cut at the bar line; `cycle=n` on a tap runs it n bars. A tap is a clock of its own (`Clock.tap`: its positions in the meter's steps, laid through the meter's time map), named `meter:tap` (`bar:16n`), its cell the meter; a tapped cell's `step.driver` is the meter and `step.tap` the tap as written. A frontend that looks a cell's clock up as a cell (`cells.get(c.clock)`) migrates to `clocks.get(c.clock).cell`.
- **`step=&bar` warns** where `&bar` is a meter ("tap the meter by pulse, beat or a note value"), naming the tap that says the same; it plays as before. **Shorten writes the migration** (`taps`, a suggestion of kind `tap`): `step=&bar*2` → `step=8n`, `&bar/4` → `1m`, `&bar*3/4` → `2t`, a meter stepped by another → `step=&swing:pulse`, offered where the piece plays the same notes. Every example and doc moved to taps this way; no example's notes moved but the two below.
- **`beat=` states a meter's beats** (alla breve: `meter("4/4") beat=2`), no longer its steps a beat; on a cell that is not a meter it does nothing, with a warning. `bpm` counts beats: with unequal beats the bar lasts beats ÷ bpm and the beats keep their proportions (7/8 at 90: a bar of 2 s, beats of 0.86, 0.57, 0.57 s). A piece that wrote `beat=` on a list or a scan migrates by hand: Swing's `&bar2 1!8 … beat=2` is `meter("4/4", 2)`, tapped `&bar2:8n` (it plays the same); a scan whose entries aren't quarters says so with **`pulse=`** (`pulse=16n`: sixteenths, its beats then quarters and its bpm counting them), so BWV 847's tempo map, `beat=4`, is `pulse=16n step=free(89bpm, 60..120)` (it plays the same, its slider, MIDI clock and MIDI file as before). The folder (MIDI → cells) writes its tempo map with `pulse=` (`16n`, or `8t` for triplets) where it wrote `beat=`, and its lines `step=pulse`.
- **`meter("7/8")` groups 3+2+2** (was 2+2+3), as decision 040's table: the Metronome's 7/8 clicks move; no other example uses it.

### Added
