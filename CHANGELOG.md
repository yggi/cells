# Changelog

What changed in the cells language, newest first: what a frontend (Cell studio, Jambot) reads before it moves its pin, `github:yggi/cells#vX.Y.Z`.
A release is a tag, and it gathers the entries under *Unreleased* beneath its own heading.
An entry says whether a piece reads and sounds as before; one that breaks a piece names its migration (Shorten writes migrations), and before 1.0 it makes the next release a minor version.

Format: [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/), as Cell studio's (lifted from yggi/cell-studio CHANGELOG.md @ 6de7ce4, its form). Add a line to *Unreleased* in the pull request that makes the change.

## Unreleased

### Added

- **cells renders audio** (decision 062, #315): `pieceAudio(result, shifts, options)` renders a piece over the span every render takes, as `pieceMidi` writes it; `createRenderer` takes events and renders frames in blocks (a stream and a file are one sound); `writeWav` writes 16- or 24-bit PCM or 32-bit float; `loudness` measures it; `npm run render -- example:<key>` writes a WAV in Node. Stems a voice each (`tracks: 'voices'`) or an entry of the sound card each (`tracks: 'entries'`), summing to the mix; an output's `parts` now say the card's entry each comes through (`entry`: its channel, its place, its cells' names). Stage R1 sounds the waveforms, noise, envelopes, levels, pan, bends, a pitch's shape and the filters (47 of the 81 examples whole); a parameter of a later stage (effects, FM, resonators, tapes) is said and rendered dry. A parameter means what superdough documents, at Cell studio's level scale. Nothing a piece plays changes, and nothing a frontend reads moves.

### Changed

- **Seven envelopes in the library, and the examples' envelopes take them** (#196, the owner's word as for the vibrato). A new fragment, `envelopes` (`fragments/expression/`, the facet `expression`), names the seven shapes the examples wrote out most, each its most common spelling: struck, nothing held, `&click` (`0 .03 0 .02`), `&tap` (`0 .08 0 .04`), `&snap` (`0 .12 0 .05`), `&thump` (`0 .3 0 .05`); held, `&strike` (`.005 .15 .5 .05`), `&pluck` (`.005 .25 .5 .1`), `&swell` (`.02 .3 .5 .3`). No name is a performer's role (`&stab`, `&pad`, `&lead`, `&keys`). An envelope within a fifth of one of them in every value (or .011 s), of the same shape, became it in the examples: 49 in 28 examples, each listed in the pull request; Normalise then takes each by name (`env=&click`, `use &click, &snap from envelopes`) in 34. The event snapshot's `notes` and `events` hashes move for the 26 whose envelopes moved. The library's own fragments keep theirs inline (`gm_kit`'s lines, which Jambot copies into its texts). The library is 76 fragments.

- **A vibrato in the library, and the examples' vibratos take it** (#196, the owner: "collapse examples with minimal differences in the parameters to the same, audio/hash changes accepted as long as the 'spirit and style of the piece' is preserved"). A new fragment, `vibrato` (`fragments/expression/`, the facet `expression`, `EXPRESSION_FRAGMENTS`), is `&vibrato map=lin loop=5 0 .1 0 -.1 0`, the vibrato the examples wrote out most: five times a second, a tenth of a semitone. Normalise writes a fragment's cell written inline (`pitch=&(map=lin loop=5 0 .1 0 -.1 0)`) as the cell by name and a `use` of it (`normalise/library.ts`). Nine examples take it: bossa, BWV 847 and pop wrote it already, and sound as before; alpha, ricercar, pachelbel, pertonos, royal and onemore wrote one near it (five to six a second, .08 to .15 of a semitone), now the library's, so their bends move a little and their event snapshot's `notes` and `events` hashes with them. A drift that takes seconds (cellscape, focus, ghosts, idm), a flutter (baustelle), a wide vibrato (spooky) and electroswing's two, each the piece's own, stay as written. The library is 75 fragments. `npm run normalise` normalises a fragment's file against the rest of the library, never as a `use` of itself. **What a frontend sees at its next pin:** a new fragment and facet; an instrument query finds `vibrato` nowhere (another facet); those nine examples have a `use &vibrato` line.

## v0.12.1 — 2026-10-29

### Added

- **Phrygian, lydian and locrian as mode fragments** (yggi/jambot#516, the owner: "jambot should be able to play all 7 modes. they should all be fragments"): with aeolian, dorian, ionian and mixolydian, every diatonic mode is a fragment, `&key scale(<mode>, &tonic)`. No preset plays them; each sits at a point of its own, placed by hand (phrygian at sync .6, harm .6, artic .7, energy .85; lydian at .15, .6, .15, .3; locrian at .9, 1, .6, .9). No example sounds different, and no event snapshot moves.

### Fixed

- **Spooky drums' rim lands on a sixteenth in every meter it fits** (yggi/jambot#521): its Euclid spread over the bar (`&euclid step=1m &rim(3,8,2)`) put the knocks between the sixteenths of every bar but 4/4's and 12/8's, so a reader on the bar's sixteenths, as Jambot's drummer is, couldn't play the fragment under 3/4, 5/4, 6/8 or 10/8, where the fit takes it. The rim now knocks once, at the middle of the bar (`&euclid step=1m [~ &rim]`), half of every bar the library's meters make being a whole number of sixteenths; a test holds it for every straight meter it fits. **What a frontend sees at its next pin:** Spooky drums read under every meter but 7/8, its rim one knock a bar where it was three. *Spooky, in pieces* plays the one knock (its event snapshot moves, 337 notes where 365), and its `postgain=` is 1.71, from Cell studio's measure (−28.6 dB); no other example moves.
- **A key's tonic read from a cell is a wire in the graph** (#290): `scale(minor, &tonic)` drew `&tonic` with no wire, unconnected; `writtenGraph` now draws a braid wire from it into the key, as from a cell the key splices in. Since v0.12.0 four examples write their key so (chords, mirrors, sympathy, worn), and every Jambot mode fragment. **What a frontend sees at its next pin:** one wire more in those graphs; nothing a piece plays changes.
- **A stack a gate plays is on the way** (#329): `routing` routed a stack's lines straight into the gate (`&kick` into `&drumsOut`), where the graph draws them into the stack (`&kick` → `&drumlines` → `&drumsOut`), so the live view never lit the stack or the wires into it. The stack is now routed, and each wire into it is routed by what the routing takes through it (`routes`), the pairs past it drawn through it (`through`). The live view of electroswing and spooky changes (`live.snapshot.txt`); nothing a piece plays changes. **What a frontend sees at its next pin:** a followed Jambot session, whose drums go through the drummer's stack, lights its stack and its lines as they play.

### Changed

- **Normalise takes the library's lines by `use`, and the examples do** (#196, `normalise/library.ts`): a line of a piece that is a library fragment's word for word, its comment aside, is written as a `use` of that fragment, one `use` a fragment where its first line was, the fragment the first in the library's order that holds the line (a line has one home), and only where it sounds identical. The library comes with the call: `normalise(source, { resolve, library })` (`NormaliseOptions`), and `npm run normalise` reads the seed library; with none given, nothing is taken. Normalise ran over the examples: 40 take their bar as `use &bar from "4/4"`, drums, midi-out and arrangement their drums from `gm_kit`, sailor its key from `dorian`. Nothing sounds different, and no event snapshot's `notes` or `events` hash moves; their `values` and `live` hashes move. The tutorial teaches `use` at tier 2, with the bar, and `meter("sig")` at tier 4 again (`ladder.ts`; `formsUsed` takes the compile's options, so a piece's `use` lines resolve). **What a frontend sees at its next pin:** 41 examples have a `use` line (electroswing had one), so an example compiled without a resolver no longer compiles; compile one as Cell studio does, with `resolveSeed`. A kit's members are listed in the order the expansion declares them, the piece's own lines first (drums: `&hh` … then `&bd`, `&sd`); each sends the note it sent.

- **Normalise writes the canonical form, and the examples are written in it** (#196, decision 049 amended: the owner, "normalize rewrites to a 'canonical form', which may alter, add, remove cells"). Two new rows (`normalise/names.ts`), each written only where it sounds identical (S1):
  - **The interface names**, where the piece names each unambiguously: its one meter `&bar` where it is a bar (its beats at 40 to 300 bpm, `BAR_BPM`), the bar's rate a cell `&tempo` (`step=120bpm` → `step=&tempo`, `&tempo step=120bpm`), its one key `&key`, the key's tonic a cell `&tonic` (`scale(minor, A)` → `scale(minor, &tonic)`, `&tonic A`); a cell with an interface name already keeps it.
  - **`scan(1 1 1 1)` as `meter("4/4")`**, the main meter of four even beats.

  Normalise ran over the examples: 64 of 80 are rewritten, and none sounds different. 59 bars name their rate `&tempo`, 45 meters are `meter("4/4")` (8 keep their scan: with it they sound otherwise), 12 keys are `&key` and 5 tonics `&tonic`; fiveto12's `&day`, studie's `&time` and BWV 847's `&tempo` are as they were. The tutorial teaches `meter("sig")` at tier 2, with the bar (`ladder.ts`), where tier 4 taught it. **What a frontend sees at its next pin:** an example's cells renamed (`&major`, `&minor`, `&scale`, `&dorian` → `&key`) and a cell more (`&tempo`, `&tonic`), so a frontend that names an example's cells finds them under their new names; the event snapshot's `values` and `live` hashes move for those 64. Its `notes` and `events` hashes move for five, by nothing heard: in swing, jazz, coltrane and bell, events that sound at one moment come in another order (`meter("4/4")` places them 1e-15 s from where the scan did); in waveforms an anonymous voice is named by its line (`λ20_14` → `λ21_14`), one line down. `normalisations` stays a factory of the compile options.
- **A recogniser keeps at most 512 texts** (#277, decision 055, point 5): `createRecogniser()` forgets the text used longest ago past its capacity, so a run of hours holds its memory and a recognition's cost (a 3-hour E45 walk kept 6,199 texts and 1.36 GB unbounded; now 512). It takes an optional `{ capacity }` (a whole number ≥ 1, or `Infinity` for the old behaviour), and returns a `KeepingRecogniser`, a `Recogniser` with `size`, the texts it keeps. `RecogniserOptions`, `KeepingRecogniser` and `RECOGNISER_CAPACITY` are exported. **What a frontend sees at its next pin:** the same answers on every recognition; Jambot's E45 can share one recogniser across its runs again. No piece sounds different.
- **The General MIDI kit is `gm_kit`, and the core kit is retired** (#196, the owner's word): a piece takes the voices it needs by name, `use &bd, &sd, &hh from gm_kit`, which the core kit, the General MIDI kit's 13 voices a groove needs written out a second time, did before a `use` could name what it takes. The library is 67 fragments; the drummer (`drums performer`) takes its 13 by name; `gm_kit` is associated at weight 1, where the core kit was, so an instrument query finds it first (`gm_kit`, `bass`, `keys`, where it found `core kit`, `gm kit`, `bass`). Both old ids still resolve, for this minor version, as fragments that only forward (`RETIRED`): `"core kit"` as `use &bd, &rim, &sd, &cp, &lt, &hh, &ph, &mt, &oh, &ht, &cr, &rd, &claves from gm_kit`, `"gm kit"` as `use * from gm_kit`, so a piece that names either plays as before. **Migration:** Normalise writes a use of a fragment that only forwards as the use it forwards to (`normalise/forwarded.ts`): `use * from "core kit"` → `use &bd, &rim, … from gm_kit`, `use &bd, &sd from "core kit"` → `use &bd, &sd from gm_kit`, `use * from "gm kit"` → `use * from gm_kit`. **What a frontend sees at its next pin:** `INSTRUMENT_FRAGMENTS` holds no `core kit` and names the kit `gm_kit`; a `use` of either old id compiles and sounds as before, its lockfile's hash now the forward's (the compile warns that it changed). Jambot's section text writes `use * from "core kit"` and its `instruments.ts` orders `core kit`, `gm kit`, `bass`; Cell studio's tests and smoke check use `"core kit"`. No example sounds different, and no event snapshot moves.

- **A heard text is compiled once** (#337, decision 055): `recognise` reads the heard text as it reads a library's fragment, kept by its text and the options, so the same text met next as a fragment (what was played lately) isn't compiled again, and Vary reads a fragment's variations in less time. Over the recorded 3-hour E45 walk the p95 bar went from 11.9 ms to 7.7 ms. **What a frontend sees at its next pin:** the same answers on every recognition, and the same variations; a heard text now takes a place among the recogniser's `size`. No piece sounds different.

### Fixed

- **A meter stepped by a rate alone is the meter MIDI follows** (#196): `mainMeter` followed `&bar … step=&tempo` up to `&tempo`, a rate with no beats of its own, so MIDI clock and a MIDI file read the rate, not the meter: an uneven scan's beats (stereo's `scan(27 23 26 24)`) came out even in the file's tempo map, and a meter's beat (`beat=2`, 6/8's three) was read as one step. Now the meter is followed, as with its rate written on it: every seed meter is written so. **What a frontend sees at its next pin:** a piece whose meter takes its rate from a cell sends the clock and writes the file it did with the rate written on the meter.
- **S1 reads an event's every number as the snapshot does** (#196): `soundsIdentical` compared an event's bends, levels, moving parameters and envelope digit for digit, where the snapshot it stands for reads every number to 9 significant digits. So one time summed two ways, 1e-15 s apart, failed it: `scan(1 1 1 1)` against `meter("4/4")` failed in 39 of the 54 examples that write it, though their snapshot hashes agree. Now 49 of the 54 pass. Four still fail because they read `&bar` as more than a meter (baustelle, effects, moving, waveforms), and tape because its physics differs by 2e-8 cents. **What a frontend sees at its next pin:** Migrate, Normalise and Shorten keep a rewrite that differed only so, where before they dropped it; nothing a piece plays changes.

### Added
- **A variation's reference** (#293, decision 052, point 7): each `Variation` Vary gives may carry `reference`: the varied line as a form on the line it varies, in forms the language already has (`form`: `&hat[<<]` for rotate, `&bassline^-1`, `inverse(&bassline)`, `&bassline[::-1]`, and `base=` for drop, add, trim and bend, `base=&hat =!6 ~`; with an anchored note, `base=&bassline^-1 0` or the line's picks around it), and `line(name)`, a cell that plays it, to read where the line was read. Absent on a Euclid, a line with a cell of levels, a rotation whose last token is a run, and where it would be longer than the line written out. Over the seed rhythms and bass lines, 472 of 535 variations have one, each playing the variation's notes (S2). **Recognition reads a reference as its path** (decision 055): a heard text that uses a fragment and writes a line it takes as one of its variations' references is that fragment by that operator, at distance 0, without a search. The type `Reference` is exported. **What a frontend sees at its next pin:** an optional field, additive; `by`, `apply` and every variation are as they were. No piece sounds different.
- **A render's span, one rule** (#315, option A): `spanOf(result, plan, { seconds, from, longest, tail })` (`@yggi/cells/engine/span`, and in `index.ts`) says how much of a piece a render takes, and why: the piece whole if it ends, one cycle of a loop (every layer back together, half an hour at most), its longest layer past that, a minute of what never comes round (`UNBOUNDED`), an hour at most (`LONGEST`), a tail of 2 s for what still rings (`TAIL`, a sound render's). `pieceMidi` reads it, and takes a span of its own as a fifth argument (`{ seconds }`). A frontend's audio render can read the same rule: Cell studio's `renderLength` caps a render at 10 minutes and a loop at one minute, which `longest` gives.
- **A MIDI file carries the main meter's signature** (#315, option A) where its beat is a quarter note (2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 5/4, a scan counted in quarters), since the file writes a beat as a quarter note; a meter whose beat is another value (6/8, 12/8, 7/8, alla breve) is written without one, as before. **What a frontend sees:** a saved MIDI file in 3/4 or 5/4 opens in a DAW with its bars, and Convert to cells cuts its key changes at bars of 3/4 or 5/4, where it took every file it saved as 4/4 (the text it writes is the same). Its notes and tempos are as they were.
- **A band: a selector over the performers** (#304 B, decision 061, amending 016 point 8): a table whose entries are performers, gates or role cells, is a band (`&band &lead &comp &bass`, no step= of its own), and a selector reading it switches their sound (`&form over=&band step=1m [0,2] [0,1,2]!3`), a role all its players, a gate named itself alone. Their parts keep their time on the meter, so they swing, and a part a text `use`s is arranged as it is used: a performer's `restart=` is `free` unless written; `restart` and `resume` work as on a section and warn off the bar. A gate in a band twice is an error, and a step= on a band warns. **`Output.select`** entries carry `performer: true` for a band's selector; the attribute role `performer` (a band's entry) takes `restart=`. **What a frontend sees at its next pin:** a selector over a table of gates, an error before ("reads mixes"), now switches them; no piece that compiled changes, and no event snapshot moves.
- **A meter's swing read from a cell: `swing=&swing`** (#308): with `&swing swing=8n:2/3`, a cell that is a swing alone, the meter swings as the swing written on it would, in every form of one (a fraction, `free(…)`, `:pairs`, several levels), as `step=&tempo` reads a rate alone (#290). Anything else read there is an error. **The four seed meters that swing** (`4/4 swung on 8n`, `4/4 leaning on 16n`, `leaning lightly`, `leaning faintly`) now write `swing=&swing` and give their share as a helper line, `&swing swing=…`: `use &bar from …` swings as before, and a text that swings otherwise names it (`use &bar, &swing from …`). Their hashes move, so a lockfile that recorded one plays the current version with a warning; each compiles to the swing it had, so **a reader that compiles a meter seed, as Jambot's `readMeterFragment` does, reads the same swing** (checked on all 13). **`use *` takes no line read only by lines the piece overrides** (a helper of the overridden line): `use * from "4/4 swung on 8n"` under the piece's own `&bar` takes no `&swing`, and says it adds nothing. The electroswing example takes its meter from the library this way, sounding identical.
- **Motifs and forms, two facets** (#294): **`motif`**, nine seeds in `fragments/motifs/` (`MOTIF_FRAGMENTS`, `@yggi/cells/repository/motifs`), each a line of one bar over the key in eighths, headed by its own name, so `use &gypsy` takes it: eight written by hand, one at each of Jambot's presets in that style's idiom and mode (`hook`, `octaves`, `sigh`, `rising`, `lazy`, `riff`, `tresillo`, `bebop`), and the electroswing example's call, `gypsy`. **`form`**, four seeds in `fragments/forms/` (`FORM_FRAGMENTS`, `@yggi/cells/repository/forms`), each a braid of holes at the centre of style space: `call and answer` (`&phrase`: the call, then `&call^-2`), `sentence`, `period`, and `AABA` (`&chorus`, its hole `&a`, its bridge `&b` A a fourth up by default). What a form derives is an operator on its hole by default, a helper a piece overrides by naming it (decision 059). Each form over each motif plays 2 or 4 bars. The library is sixty-eight. Jambot queries neither facet, so nothing it plays changes.
- **Vary reads a held note** (#294, decision 052): a degree held, `7@2`, is one note two steps long, varied by transpose and invert and written back held; reverse, bend and trim leave a line with a held note alone. So a motif that holds its notes is recognised (decision 055): the answer of `call and answer`, heard alone, is its motif `transpose -2`. A line that was unread is now read; no line that was read is varied differently.
- **A new example, `electroswing`: Electro swing, in pieces** (#196's scouting for rung 4): a house kick under a walking bass, Charleston stabs, and a clarinet and a muted trumpet trading a call and its answer in D harmonic minor, 32 bars. The first example that `use`s the seed library (the drums performer and its core kit, `harmonic minor` on the piece's `&tonic`, `vamp to the fifth`, `walking bass`, the bass, stab and lead performers, their instruments given a sound of the piece's own, and the beat and the stabs it gave the library, the drummer playing them as a stack each times its level in the form) and the first literate one (prose between its blocks, decision 051). Its tune is one bar moved by operators (`^n`, `degree(…)`, `[::-1]`) and cut by picks. A frontend shows it at its next pin; it compiles only with a resolver (`resolveSeed`), as Cell studio's does. Since #294 its tune is the seed motif `gypsy` in the forms `call and answer` and `AABA`, its bridge overridden, the call on the clarinet and the answer on the trumpet.
- **Three seeds from the example, and a facet: `stab`** (the owner's word on #305): `Electro swing drums` (`fragments/rhythms/`, a rhythm), `harmonic minor` (`fragments/modes/`, `&key scale(harmonic, &tonic)`) and `Charleston stabs` (`fragments/stab/`, the stab performer's `&stabline`, its last stab the next chord's, `STAB_FRAGMENTS` from `@yggi/cells/repository/stab` and `index.ts`). All three sit at a point proposed for a ninth preset, Electro swing (sync .55, harm .75, artic .6, energy .75), written in their files: Jambot's to adopt, with no constant beside its presets (decision 041). The library is fifty-five. **What Jambot sees at its next pin:** at its eight presets every facet's first fragment is what it was (all 56 queries, each at distance 0); between them, 8 of 2156 points sampled along each pair of presets now find a new seed first (`Electro swing drums` and `harmonic minor`, between Bossa nova and Deep house and between Funk and Swing jazz). Its composer plays no harmonic minor (not among `COMPOSER_MODES`), so only the drums would reach it there.
- **A gate plays a stack of lines, and the drums performer** (#287, decision 058): `&drums play=&drumlines &kit`, where `&drumlines [&kick, &backbeat, &hat]` is a chord of lines, plays each line as it would play it alone, on each of its voices at its level, and moved where the gate moves (`play=&both^$-12`). Each hit keeps its line's name in its output (`kit:voices:kick:…`), the drum role feel v1.1 reads. A line may name its own voices (a groove: `&kick step=16n &bd:0:.6 ~ ~ &bd`), each hit with its own gain, Strudel's `s("bd:0:.6")`; played on a kit it strikes only the kit's voices (an error names the others). A stack a gate plays is its part, not a sound: mixing or sending it beside the gate is an error. ### Changed
- **Taps by pulse, beat and note value; a tap comes round at the bar** (#74, decision 040, rung 1 of #15). A compiled `Meter` knows its pulse (`pulses`, the numerator), its beats (`beats`, each in pulses: 6/8 `3 3`, 7/8 `3 2 2`) and the note value of a pulse (`unit`, the denominator; a scan's is 4). A line taps it: `step=pulse`, `step=pulse*k`, `step=beat`, `step=beat*k`, note values `1n` … `32n`, triplets `4t 8t 16t`, dotted `2n. 4n. 8n.`, the bar `1m`, with a chain as on any clock (`1m/4`); of the main meter (the first in the text with a tempo of its own, `CompileResult.mainMeter`) or a named one (`step=&bar2:16n`). A note value longer than what is left of the bar is cut at the bar line; `cycle=n` on a tap runs it n bars. A tap is a clock of its own (`Clock.tap`: its positions in the meter's steps, laid through the meter's time map), named `meter:tap` (`bar:16n`), its cell the meter; a tapped cell's `step.driver` is the meter and `step.tap` the tap as written. A frontend that looks a cell's clock up as a cell (`cells.get(c.clock)`) migrates to `clocks.get(c.clock).cell`.
- **`step=&bar` warns** where `&bar` is a meter ("tap the meter by pulse, beat or a note value"), naming the tap that says the same; it plays as before. **Shorten writes the migration** (`taps`, a suggestion of kind `tap`): `step=&bar*2` → `step=8n`, `&bar/4` → `1m`, `&bar*3/4` → `2t`, a meter stepped by another → `step=&swing:pulse`, offered where the piece plays the same notes. Every example and doc moved to taps this way; no example's notes moved but the two below.
- **`beat=` states a meter's beats** (alla breve: `meter("4/4") beat=2`), no longer its steps a beat; on a cell that is not a meter it does nothing, with a warning. `bpm` counts beats: with unequal beats the bar lasts beats ÷ bpm and the beats keep their proportions (7/8 at 90: a bar of 2 s, beats of 0.86, 0.57, 0.57 s). A piece that wrote `beat=` on a list or a scan migrates by hand: Swing's `&bar2 1!8 … beat=2` is `meter("4/4", 2)`, tapped `&bar2:8n` (it plays the same); a scan whose entries aren't quarters says so with **`pulse=`** (`pulse=16n`: sixteenths, its beats then quarters and its bpm counting them), so BWV 847's tempo map, `beat=4`, is `pulse=16n step=free(89bpm, 60..120)` (it plays the same, its slider, MIDI clock and MIDI file as before). The folder (MIDI → cells) writes its tempo map with `pulse=` (`16n`, or `8t` for triplets) where it wrote `beat=`, and its lines `step=pulse`.
- **`meter("7/8")` groups 3+2+2** (was 2+2+3), as decision 040's table: the Metronome's 7/8 clicks move; no other example uses it.

### Added
- **The package ships `docs/language/`** (the topic pages and the generated syntax reference), and `npm run reference` rewrites the reference and the README's index of forms. No piece reads or sounds differently, and nothing in `src/` changes; an install is 119 KB larger unpacked (about 43 KB gzipped), the pages and the reference, and the package's tests hold it to at most 150 KB.
